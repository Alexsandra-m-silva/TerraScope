01 / The challenge

Land degradation is an environmental issue—and a human one.

Desertification and soil degradation can reduce agricultural productivity, intensify water stress, and weaken the resilience of communities and food systems. Understanding where pressures are emerging is an important step toward responding effectively.

⌁

Environmental degradation

Changes in vegetation, soil moisture, and land conditions can signal growing pressure on ecosystems and productive landscapes.

♧

Agricultural and water stress

Drought, declining soil conditions, and water scarcity can threaten crops, livestock, livelihoods, and local food availability.

◎

Community vulnerability

Environmental shocks can have greater consequences where poverty, limited resources, or barriers to food access already exist.

02 / Our approach

Turn multiple data sources into a clearer picture of risk.

TerraScope is designed to combine Earth observation with climate, agricultural, and socioeconomic information. AI-supported analysis can reveal patterns and flag unusual changes—while local data and expert validation remain essential.

01 / OBSERVE

Collect geospatial data

Use satellite observations alongside climate, land, and agricultural datasets.

02 / ANALYZE

Detect environmental change

Analyze vegetation, surface conditions, water dynamics, and land use over time.

03 / CONNECT

Assess human impacts

Combine environmental signals with agricultural and socioeconomic indicators.

04 / ACT

Support targeted action

Prioritize areas for investigation, prevention, restoration, and resilience.

Satellite observations do not directly measure food insecurity. TerraScope would estimate vulnerability by combining environmental indicators with appropriate local household, agricultural, and socioeconomic data.

03 / A proposed analytical component

Food Vulnerability Index

A proposed composite indicator to help identify places where environmental pressures may increase the risk of food insecurity. It connects environmental exposure with agricultural sensitivity and community-level vulnerability.

The index would be developed and validated with appropriate local data. It is intended as a decision-support tool—not a direct measurement of household food insecurity.

05 / Data and technology

Built on complementary sources, not a single satellite.

NISAR is one potential source of Earth-observation information. Its radar measurements can help monitor land and vegetation structure; other datasets add context for food vulnerability and restoration outcomes.

◉

Earth observation

NASA–ISRO NISAR, Sentinel missions, Landsat, MODIS, and other suitable sources.

☁

Climate and agriculture

Rainfall, temperature, drought, crop production, land use, and water resources.

▤

Socioeconomic context

Population, income, poverty, food access, and locally relevant public statistics.

Data sources, spatial resolution, update frequency, and methods depend on the pilot region and data availability.
